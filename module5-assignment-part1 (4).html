Example:

a) 2021 Trend: Cyber attacks have evolved and become more frequent.

b) Current challenge: In 2026, AI has become very advanced. In October 2026, supermarket fridges in France were hacked, food defrosted and had to be discarded.

c) Source: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crq5nzxv9q4yo

PESTLE+V analysis

The prompt questions will guide you. Click each letter to open the example.

P Political

How is the government responding?

  • France ordered a cybersecurity investigation and is working with EU partners to assess the risk to other companies.
  • Governments are under pressure to protect “critical infrastructure”, and food supply may now count as part of it.
E Economic

What are the direct and indirect costs?

  • Direct: Shoppers faced empty shelves and worried about how reliable their food supply is.
  • Direct: The insurance cost of spoiled food.
  • Indirect: Food prices go up.
S Social

How do consumers feel about the situation? What are their current habits?

  • Shoppers faced empty shelves and worried about how reliable their food supply is.
  • People currently trust “smart” technology.
T Technological

How has technology contributed to the issue? How are tech companies responding?

  • Connected (“smart”) fridges are part of the Internet of Things, and every connected device is a possible entry point for hackers.
  • Advanced AI could help attackers find weaknesses faster.
L Legal

What laws already exist?

  • Companies may be held responsible for failing to protect customers and food safety.
  • EU cybersecurity and data rules, and food hygiene law, may be tightened or enforced more strictly.
E Environmental

What is the environmental cost?

  • Hundreds of tons of food were spoiled, which means wasted water, energy, land and transport as well as the food itself.
  • Food waste adds to greenhouse gas emissions, which works against sustainability goals.
  • Replacing damaged fridges and restocking adds even more resource use.
V Values

Which values are involved?

  • Safety: people have a right to feel safe in shops and to trust that their food is safe.
  • Responsibility: who is accountable? The retailers, the equipment makers, the hackers, or the governments?
  • Resilience: do societies value being prepared for failure, or only efficiency and low cost?
  • Trust and transparency: companies and governments need to be honest about attacks, even when it is uncomfortable.
SDG UN Sustainable Development Goals

How is the situation linked to the UN SDGs?

  • The situation relates to SDGs 9 (Industry, innovation and infrastructure), 10 (Reduced inequalities) and 11 (Sustainable cities and communities).
